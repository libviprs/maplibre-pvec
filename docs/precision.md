# Precision

GPU `float32` has a 24-bit mantissa. A survey coordinate of 1e7 units resolves to a fraction of a metre, which at zoom 22 is many device pixels. So I never hand the GPU a large world coordinate. Canonical PVec coordinates stay `f64` and are untouched.

## The scheme

1. Source coordinates go through the calibration, the CRS and the mercator forward transform in `f64`, giving normalised world coordinates.
2. Each spatial block gets a `LocalOrigin` (the centre of its bounds, snapped to a `2^-24` grid so identical bounds always give the identical origin), held in `f64`.
3. Vertex attributes are `world - origin` computed in `f64`, then split with `splitHighLow`: `hi = Math.fround(v)`, `lo = v - hi`.
4. Every frame the CPU computes `camera - origin` in `f64`, splits it, and uploads it as `uOriginHigh` / `uOriginLow`.
5. `src/render/shaders/position.glsl` reconstructs `(hi - originHi) + (lo - originLo)`, the position relative to the camera, before any projection.

Block origins are a pure function of block bounds, so a rebase cannot move a vertex between two frames at the same camera state. The test pans 10 000 px across 40 origin changes and the largest displacement between two origin choices is below 1e-3 px (limit 0.05).

## Measured error

These numbers come from `tests/unit/transform/stress.test.ts`, which emulates the vertex shader with `Math.fround` on every operation and compares against an `f64` projection of the same vertices. The table is regenerated by that test and compared against this file, so it cannot drift from the measurement. Setup: source magnitude 1e7 mm, blocks of plus or minus 5 km, `devicePixelRatio` 2, worst case over feature size (1e-3 to 1e2), latitude (0 to 84), plan rotation (0 to 89.9) and bearing/pitch (0/0 to 135/60).

Reading it: naive single-`f32` world coordinates are already 16.9 px off at zoom 18 and 346 px off at zoom 22. A local origin alone gets inside 0.25 px up to zoom 22 for blocks of plus or minus 5 km, but it is at 0.21 px there and 0.79 px at zoom 24, so there is no headroom for bigger blocks. The high/low split keeps the worst case near 5e-4 px. That is why every geometry program uses it.

## Envelope

| quantity | gated limit |
|---|---|
| zoom | 22 |
| source magnitude | 1e7 units |
| latitude | 84 degrees |

Inside the envelope the full 33 600-cell matrix (7 zooms, 4 magnitudes, 4 feature sizes, 5 latitudes, 5 plan rotations, 4 bearing/pitch pairs, 3 pixel ratios) stays at or below 1.3e-3 px against a 0.25 px limit. Zoom 24 is recorded (worst 1.1e-3 px in the emulation) but labelled outside the v1 envelope and never gates. Outside the envelope `checkPrecisionEnvelope` returns a `precision-envelope` diagnostic (a 1e9-magnitude coordinate is the tested case) instead of degrading silently.

## Rasters

A raster quad's corners go through the chain the strokes use: source to world in f64, the hi and lo split, and the view linearised at the centre of the canvas. At pitch 0 the linearisation is exact, so a corner lands on `map.project` to 1e-11 device px (arithmetic, not a pixel measurement), and the scan edge and a stroke along it coincide within 0.1 device px at zooms 0 to 4, bearings 0 and 30, pixel ratios 1 and 2 (worst 0.097 px, MapLibre 4.7.1, software GL; the drawing is kept at 160 px across by scaling the calibration with the zoom, so the stress is the size of the world coordinates, not the size on screen).

Under pitch the layer linearises at the centre of the canvas, as the strokes do, so a corner away from the centre lands where the tilted projection does not put it. Corner landing error against `map.project`, a 160 px square at zoom 2, pixel ratio 1, identical on 4.7.1, 5.24.0 and 6.16.0:

| pitch | corners (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right), px |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.00, 0.00, 0.00, 0.00 |
| 30 | 14.07, 14.07, 7.67, 7.67 |
| 60 | 16.55, 16.55, 14.07, 14.07 |

Nothing is promised under pitch beyond what the strokes promise; a projection-matrix path is a separate decision.

## What this does not prove

The emulation assumes the GPU does `highp` single-precision arithmetic with correct rounding per operation. Real drivers can fuse multiply-adds or use different `sin`/`cos` approximations, so the browser run on real GL contexts (`docs/deferred.md`) is still needed before the numbers are quoted as GPU measurements. The 0.25 px gate and the 8 px naive-error claim are asserted against the emulation today.
